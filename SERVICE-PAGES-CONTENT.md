### Section 2: What Auto Insurance Coverage Is
Auto insurance is a contract between you and an insurance company that protects you financially if you're involved in an accident or your vehicle is damaged or stolen. Your policy is made up of different types of coverage, each protecting you in different situations.

**Liability Coverage:**
- **Bodily Injury Liability:** Covers medical expenses, lost wages, and legal fees if you injure someone in an accident
- **Property Damage Liability:** Covers damage you cause to someone else's vehicle or property
- **South Dakota Minimum:** $25,000 per person / $50,000 per accident for bodily injury, $25,000 for property damage

**Physical Damage Coverage:**
- **Collision:** Covers damage to your vehicle from accidents, regardless of fault
- **Comprehensive:** Covers damage from theft, vandalism, weather, fire, and other non-collision events

**Additional Protection:**
- **Uninsured/Underinsured Motorist:** Protects you if you're hit by a driver with no insurance or insufficient coverage
- **Medical Payments:** Covers medical expenses for you and your passengers
- **Roadside Assistance:** Help with towing, flat tires, dead batteries, and lockouts

**Common Misconceptions:**
- "I only need the minimum required coverage" - Minimum coverage may not be enough to protect your assets if you're at fault in a serious accident
- "Full coverage means everything is covered" - "Full coverage" typically means liability + collision + comprehensive, but doesn't cover everything
- "My rates will go up if I file a claim" - Not always true; it depends on the circumstances and your policy

### Section 2: What Home Insurance Coverage Is
Home insurance (also called homeowners insurance) is a package policy that combines several types of coverage into one policy. It protects your home, your personal belongings, and your liability if someone is injured on your property.

**Dwelling Coverage:**
- Protects the structure of your home (walls, roof, foundation, built-in appliances)
- Should be enough to rebuild your home at current construction costs
- Covers damage from fire, wind, hail, lightning, and other covered perils

**Personal Property Coverage:**
- Protects your belongings (furniture, electronics, clothing, etc.)
- Typically 50-70% of your dwelling coverage amount
- Covers theft and damage from covered perils
- May have limits on high-value items like jewelry or art

**Liability Coverage:**
- Protects you if someone is injured on your property
- Covers legal fees and medical expenses
- Typically starts at $100,000, but we recommend $300,000-$500,000

**Additional Living Expenses:**
- Covers hotel stays, meals, and other costs if your home is uninhabitable after a covered loss
- Typically 10-20% of dwelling coverage

**Common Misconceptions:**
- "My home is insured for what I paid for it" - You need enough coverage to rebuild, which may be more or less than what you paid
- "Flood damage is covered" - Standard home insurance doesn't cover floods; you need separate flood insurance
- "Everything I own is fully covered" - High-value items may need additional coverage (scheduled personal property)

Life insurance is a contract between you and an insurance company. You pay premiums, and in exchange, the company pays a death benefit to your beneficiaries if you die while the policy is in force.

**Term Life Insurance:**
- Provides coverage for a specific period (10, 20, or 30 years)
- Most affordable option

- Ideal for temporary needs like mortgage protection or children's education
- Premiums typically fixed for the term period

**Whole Life Insurance:**
- Provides coverage for your entire life
- Builds cash value over time
- Premiums typically fixed and higher than term
- Can be used as an investment/savings tool
- Guaranteed death benefit

**Universal Life Insurance:**
- Flexible permanent life insurance
- Adjustable premiums and death benefits
- Builds cash value
- More flexible than whole life

**Common Misconceptions:**
- "Life insurance is too expensive" - Term life insurance is often more affordable than people think, especially when you're young and healthy

### Section 2: What Health Insurance Coverage Is
Health insurance is a contract between you and an insurance company that helps pay for medical expenses. You pay premiums, and in exchange, the insurance company helps cover the cost of medical care.

**Types of Health Insurance:**
- **Individual Health Insurance:** Coverage for you and/or your family
- **Group Health Insurance:** Coverage through an employer or organization
- **Medicare Supplements:** Additional coverage for Medicare beneficiaries
- **Short-Term Health Insurance:** Temporary coverage for gaps in coverage

**Key Coverage Components:**
- **Premiums:** Monthly payments you make to keep your coverage
- **Deductible:** Amount you pay out-of-pocket before insurance starts paying
- **Copayments:** Fixed amount you pay for specific services (like doctor visits)
- **Coinsurance:** Percentage you pay for services after meeting your deductible
- **Out-of-Pocket Maximum:** Maximum amount you'll pay in a year

**Common Plan Types:**
- **HMO (Health Maintenance Organization):** Lower costs, must use network providers
- **PPO (Preferred Provider Organization):** More flexibility, can see out-of-network providers
- **EPO (Exclusive Provider Organization):** Network-only coverage, no referrals needed
- **High-Deductible Health Plans (HDHP):** Lower premiums, higher deductibles, often paired with HSAs

**Common Misconceptions:**
- "I'm healthy, so I don't need health insurance" - Accidents and unexpected illnesses happen to everyone
- "Health insurance is too expensive" - There are options for various budgets, and going without can cost much more
- "All health insurance plans are the same" - Plans vary significantly in coverage, costs, and provider networks
